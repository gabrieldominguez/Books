gif vs jpeg

At present, Web browsers can only view two types of images, both are bitmapped:

gif (Graphics Interchange Format)

  • This is a lossless compressed eight bit graphic format displaying 256 colors.
  • It was developed by Compuserve. Developers have to pay royalties for its use, although as end users we do not.
  • There has been much discussion about improving the gif file format, but the problem is obtaining universal acceptability.

jpeg or jpg (Joint Photographic Experts Group)

  • This lossy compressed bitmapped file format displaying 16.7 million colors. The image quality lost depends on the degree of compression set.
  • Some graphic manipulation programs such as Photoshop, let you choose the amount of image degredation.
  • The jpeg file sizes may be smaller than that of the gif, depending on the degree of compression and size of the colour palette.
  • You may see a file extension jpe if you are viewing a file that has been downloaded from UNIX and had its four character extension (jpeg) truncated, to fit the DOS three character extension.

When to use GIF

  • Small, iconic and thumbnail images
  • Graphics, logos, logotype
  • Flat colour areas
  • Some small area photographic or continuous tone images
  • When you absolutely want to support every browser out there
  • animations

When to use a JPEG

  • Some small area photographic or continuous tone Images
  • Large area photographic or continuous tone Images
  • When *.jpeg is smaller than *.gif
  • Lots and lots of colors
